01 / A&R brief

2-step pop with R&B vocals.

The song pairs REI's conversational delivery with LIZ's sustained melodic phrases. REI introduces the track in the first eight seconds; LIZ leads the pre-chorus. Alternating lines and a shared chorus phrase establish the duet.

Composition

Tempo / meter
Target: 114 BPM in 4/4. Working range: 112-116 BPM.
Key options
Start in F# minor, with an A-major emphasis in the chorus. Alternative: B minor / D major. Confirm a comfortable singing range with guide vocals before choosing the final key.
Verse
F#m9 / Dmaj7 / Aadd9 / E6
Pre-chorus
Dmaj7 / E6 / C#m7 / F#m9
Chorus
Aadd9 / E/G# / F#m9 / Dmaj7

These progressions illustrate the intended sound. Writers may develop other harmonies; a key change is optional.

Arrangement and sound

Drums
Syncopated 2-step kick, slightly delayed offbeat closed hi-hats and a soft garage snare. Add quiet rim clicks in the verses and wider stereo claps in the final chorus.
Bass
Sub-bass with short, syncopated notes under REI's verses and longer notes under LIZ's pre-choruses.
Texture
Filtered pads, a single bright plucked motif, subtle vocal chops and light tape saturation.
Vocal space
Keep the lead vocals upfront with short, restrained reverb. Leave room for clear consonants and avoid large risers or a heavy drop.

LIZ / Pre-chorus lead

Lead the longer melodic phrases with a clear vocal tone. Spread the double-tracked vocals across the stereo field in the pre-chorus. Add a light upper harmony and one restrained ad-lib in the final chorus.

REI / Verse lead

Use a dry, conversational vocal, with phrasing between singing and rap in the second verse. Add a quiet lower-register double and short whispered accents while keeping her lead voice clear.

Lyric direction

Set the lyric after a night out: one person wants to stay, the other is ready to leave. Give each verse a different point of view. The chorus turns their disagreement into a shared decision to take the same way home.

Treat this as a fictional song situation. Keep the language conversational and specific; the lyric should stand on its own without relying on knowledge of the members' relationship. Writers may propose a different scene that preserves two perspectives and a shared choice.

Listening references

  • IVE / Off The Record: An intimate, after-hours atmosphere.
  • aespa / Thirsty: Smooth R&B delivery and layered harmonies.
  • PinkPantheress / Pain: A skipping groove beneath a close vocal.

Use these references to discuss atmosphere, rhythm and vocal production. The song requires its own melody, harmony and sound design.

Demo commission and selection

Commission three complete topline demos from writers experienced in R&B vocals and 2-step programming. Request a guide for both voices, a 15-second chorus edit, lyrics, BPM, key, bar-by-bar structure, writer contacts and a proposed publishing split.

Shortlist demos for vocal contrast, live performance feasibility, a memorable chorus and replay value. Test the shortlisted songs in a seated vocal session and then with stage blocking before making the final selection.

Selected demo deliverables

Deliver the full mix, instrumental, clean vocal guide and individual stems as 24-bit / 48 kHz WAV files, all starting at bar one. Include dry leads and doubles, harmony stems, and a declaration of any samples or interpolations. Prepare separate concert playback and streaming masters.

Avoid copying an IVE title-track formula, adding an unrelated rap break or heavy drop, or processing the two voices until they sound alike. Confirm the key, tempo and budget with the artists and production team.

02 / Music direction & session plan

Rhythm, vocal testing and mix review.

Program the rhythm, rehearse the chorus with the proposed blocking and revise any parts that mask the vocals. Confirm the final key and vocal parts with the artists during the session.

Optional four-bar rhythm sketch
4 bars · 8.4 seconds

Original synthesized drums, bass and chords at 114 BPM. No artist recordings or vocals; this is a rhythm reference.

01

Program a restrained 2-step rhythm.

At 114 BPM in 4/4, divide one bar into 16 sixteenth-note steps. Place the kick on 1, 2&, 3& (steps 1, 7, 11), the soft snare on 2, 4 (steps 5, 13), and closed hi-hats on every eighth note (steps 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15). Delay only the four offbeat hi-hats by 18 ms rather than applying swing to the whole pattern.

Keep onbeat hi-hats quieter than offbeats. Use short sub-bass notes under the conversational verses and longer notes through the pre-chorus. Remove any kick that masks a vocal pickup. Add rim clicks in the verses and claps in the final chorus without changing the main backbeat.

The site's audio player contains an original four-bar synthesized sketch of the drums, bass and verse chords. It demonstrates the proposed rhythm and arrangement space. It is not a finished demo and contains no artist vocals, artist recordings or samples from the reference tracks.

02

Give the chorus a brighter harmonic emphasis.

Verse: F#m9 / Dmaj7 / Aadd9 / E6. Chorus: Aadd9 / E/G# / F#m9 / Dmaj7. F# minor and A major share a key signature. Starting the chorus on A can suggest a brighter tonal center, although these chords alone do not establish a modulation. Spread the upper chord tones and sustain the bass as the singers begin their first shared phrase.

Give REI short rhythmic phrases with space for replies, and give LIZ longer phrases and wider double-tracked vocals in the pre-chorus. Keep both lead vocals centered. Test the upper harmonies and octave choices in the session before assigning them.

03

Choose the key through vocal and movement tests.

Start with the F#-minor guide. Mark each chorus note, syllable, duration and breath point. Test complete phrases, including repeated high notes and quiet low entries. Choose a range the singers can repeat comfortably, rather than judging it by a single high note.

  1. Seated test: record three full chorus takes over the instrumental, capturing dry vocals with no tuned lead in the playback. Ask each singer to identify any strain or difficult breath points.
  2. Movement test: sing three more takes while following the proposed blocking, resting for 45 seconds between takes. Keep the guide melody and tempo the same.
  3. Comparison: if an upper phrase repeatedly causes tension, try it one semitone lower. If low entries repeatedly lose clarity, try it one semitone higher. Retest the whole chorus; adjust sustained notes or breath points if transposition creates other difficulties.
  4. Approval: require three consecutive movement takes with the intended melody, clear lyrics and planned breaths, without reported strain or unplanned octave changes. The singers and vocal director agree on the comfortable working range.

F# minor and the B-minor alternative remain provisional. Public clips are insufficient to confirm the final key, octaves or upper harmonies.

03 / Wardrobe direction

Black leather and silver detail.

For the proposed 2-step pop and R&B performance, use fitted black garments with a defined waist and small areas of reflective trim. The reference photographs show garments and construction details from Maison de VYNN.

Silhouette

Keep the shoulder line clear. Compare an asymmetric one-sleeve top with a straight-neck bodice and trousers. Use the contrast in cut to distinguish the two artists while keeping the material palette consistent.

Surface and detail

Combine a black leather base, reflective panels and silver crystal work. Concentrate embellishment at the neckline or cuff. Test how the texture, trim and waist shaping read together in a full-body two-shot.

04 / Performance pilot

Test demand before scheduling a release.

Use the first performances to assess demand for the unit and the song. Review the initial response after 72 hours, then confirm it at day seven before deciding on a release.

Responsibilities and scheduling

Artist Strategy leads the proposal, comparison set and release recommendation. A&R manages the demo, vocal tests and master preparation. Performance, Content and Marketing confirm rehearsal capacity, filming plans and access to the required data.

After Management confirms the group schedule and rehearsal capacity, secure two feasible tour slots in different markets. Existing tour dates are planning options until a unit performance is approved.

Minimum production scope

Produce one song, one blocking plan, one LED graphic system and one coordinated wardrobe concept. Film a two-shot master and angles suitable for vertical edits. Set a fixed rehearsal allocation. Consider a standalone music video and full unit campaign after the pilot meets the approval criteria.

This plan assumes access to existing tour production and official-channel analytics, including organic viewer counts by market for the comparison clips. Set the budget using supplier quotes and the internal resources required.

  1. Perform: present the unreleased song as a special stage. The members' separate paths meet in the final chorus.
  2. Publish: allow initial fan discovery, then post one 12-18 second official chorus edit without announcing a release date.
  3. Measure: collect consistent 24-hour and 72-hour data after each official post. Recheck both markets at day seven.
  4. Review: Artist Strategy reviews the three demand indicators with A&R, Marketing and Management. Hold the release decision if required evidence is missing.

Demand indicators

01 / Chorus reuse
Count unique creators posting a new performance, cover or interpretation of the original chorus. Report creators per 1,000 organic viewers of the corresponding official edit. Use the same platform, observation window and independently verified market for creators and viewers. Hold the decision if market-level viewer counts are unavailable. Deduplicate reposts and exclude official, paid and coordinated campaign accounts.
02 / LIZ + REI discussion
Compare the mean daily number of unique authors discussing LIZ + REI before and after the performances, using the same fixed query, platform and verified market. In both periods, exclude paid or coordinated activity, repost-only accounts, group-only discussion and discussion limited to the members' relationship. Separately identify authors who explicitly discuss the proposed song, chorus or performance and report their share.
03 / Release requests
Count unique users explicitly requesting a studio or digital release of this song. Report users per 1,000 organic viewers of the corresponding official edit, using the same market, platform and observation window. Hold the decision if the viewer denominator is unavailable.

Comparison set and approval criteria

Before the pilot, select five recent official performance clips from the same channel, matched for length, promotion and tour context. Keep this comparison set fixed. Compare posts at the same time after publication and use the median rather than the best-performing clip.

In both markets, chorus creators and release-request users per 1,000 organic viewers must exceed their respective matched medians. Match the response counts and viewer denominators by platform, verified market and elapsed observation window. These rates adjust for exposure; they do not show how many of the actual viewers converted.

For LIZ + REI discussion, compare the mean daily author count in the first 72 hours with the daily mean from the seven days before the performances. Require growth in both markets and require song-related discussion to account for more than half of eligible author-days after the performances. Calculate sum of daily song-related authors / sum of daily eligible LIZ + REI authors > 50%, counting each author once per day in each total. Agree on this proposed threshold before the pilot. Review other activity that may have contributed to the increase.

At day seven, recalculate the daily author mean and song-related share across all seven days after the performances. Recalculate both response rates using seven-day viewer counts and compare them with the fixed clips at the same time after publication. All three indicators must continue to pass.

Verify markets using platform audience geography or independently verified local channels. Language alone is insufficient. Small samples, missing viewer counts or incomplete location data do not meet the approval criteria.

Proceed: schedule the digital single when all three demand indicators and evidence checks pass in both markets, and the artists, master and activity schedule are approved.

Hold: keep the song as a tour performance and collect more data if evidence is weak or incomplete, response spikes in only one market or channel, or group activity appears to drive the response.

Revise the song: if interest in the pair is strong but chorus reuse and release requests are weak, commission a revised demo and repeat the pilot. Do not increase the release budget before testing the revised demo.

After release approval

Support the single with a performance video, visualizer, recording behind-the-scenes footage and a short interview with both members. Continue the visual direction established on stage. During the pilot, prepare material within the approved scope and announce a release date after the decision.

At 28 days after release, compare saves per listener, repeat listening where available, continued creator reuse and response to unit content with similar releases. Use findings from the pilot to inform future IVE group projects. Commission a follow-up when sustained demand and schedule capacity are confirmed.

The thresholds and observation windows are proposed planning rules, not Starship's internal standards or measured campaign results. The relevant teams make the final decision together.
