01 / A&R brief

Velvet 2-Step Pop.

Write a close, rhythmic unit song built around two distinct voices. REI establishes character in the opening eight seconds. LIZ opens the melodic space. Their first shared hook makes the pair legible.

Musical specification

Tempo / meter
114 BPM preferred; 112-116 BPM range. 4/4.
Key candidates
F# minor with an A-major color in the hook. Backup: B minor / D major. Confirm tessitura with guide vocals before locking the key.
Verse
F#m9 / Dmaj7 / Aadd9 / E6
Pre-chorus
Dmaj7 / E6 / C#m7 / F#m9
Chorus
Aadd9 / E/G# / F#m9 / Dmaj7

Chord colors are a writing lane, not a mandatory harmonic template or a required modulation.

Production specification

Drums
Broken 2-step kick, shuffled closed hats, soft garage snare. Verse rim-click detail; wider clap in the final hook.
Bass
Elastic sub. Short syncopation under REI; longer notes under LIZ's pre-chorus.
Texture
Filtered pads, one glassy pluck motif, low-level vocal chops and light tape air.
Space
Close lead vocals, restrained reverb tails, separation around consonants. No oversized riser or heavy drop.

LIZ / Line & lift

Clean lead and longer melodic phrases. Widen doubles in the pre-chorus. Add an airy upper harmony and one restrained ad-lib in the final hook.

REI / Texture & hook

Dry, conversational lead; rap-sung phrasing in verse two. Use a subtle lower-color double and whispered tags without obscuring her natural tone.

76 bars / 2:40 at 114 BPM

Hook direction

Different taste, same side
You go left, I go right
Still we meet on the same line

Draft writing prompt, not final lyrics. The hook should make sense without an explanation of the members' relationship.

Reference functions

  • IVE / Off The Record: private, elegant temperature.
  • aespa / Thirsty: smooth vocal layering.
  • NMIXX / Passionfruit: light UKG bounce.
  • PinkPantheress: close vocal and compact movement.

These are listening directions, not templates for melody, chords, or signature sounds.

Supply request

Request three full topline demos from writers with R&B vocal and 2-step programming experience. Each submission must include a two-voice guide, a 15-second hook cut, lyric sheet, BPM/key/bar map, writer contacts and split proposal.

Shortlist on role contrast, live singability, hook recall, and replay value. Run a seated vocal check and a movement rehearsal before the final selection.

Approved-demo delivery

Full mix, instrumental, clean vocal guide and aligned stems: 24-bit / 48 kHz WAV from bar one. Include dry lead/doubles, harmony stacks and a sample/interpolation declaration. Keep the concert playback version separate from the streaming master.

No title-track imitation, inserted rap break, heavy drop or twin-voice treatment. Key, tempo and budget remain subject to artist and production approval.

02 / Music direction & session decisions

Turn the brief into a playable decision.

A proposed session plan: hear the groove, test the line in the body, then revise only what blocks the hook. No artist vocal range, rehearsal result or finished recording is claimed.

01

Program the bounce; leave the backbeat alone.

At 114 BPM in 4/4, start with one bar of 16 sixteenths. Kick: 1, 2&, 3& (steps 1, 7, 11). Soft snare: 2, 4 (steps 5, 13). Closed hats: every eighth (steps 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15). Delay only the four offbeat hats by 18 ms; this is a local timing offset, not global swing.

Keep onbeat hats quieter than offbeats. Shorten the sub notes under conversational phrases; lengthen them through the pre-chorus. Remove a kick if it crowds a vocal pickup. Verse rim detail and a final-hook clap are additions to the backbeat, not a new drop.

The site audition is an original synthesized four-bar sketch of drums, bass and the verse chord lane. It contains no member vocal, artist recording or reference-song sample. Its purpose is to hear timing and space, not to represent a finished demo.

02

Open the hook with relative-major color.

Verse: F#m9 / Dmaj7 / Aadd9 / E6. Hook: Aadd9 / E/G# / F#m9 / Dmaj7. F# minor and A major share a key signature. An A-led hook suggests a brighter center; the chord list alone does not prove modulation. Open the upper chord voicing, hold the bass longer and let the first shared vowel carry the lift.

REI: propose dry rhythmic phrasing with small response gaps. LIZ: propose longer phrases with widened doubles in the pre-chorus. Keep both leads centered. Test upper harmonies and octave choices in the session; these roles describe the arrangement, not their measured ranges.

03

Lock tessitura after the movement test.

Start with the F#-minor guide. Mark every hook note, its syllable, duration and breath point. Test the whole phrase, including repeated high notes and quiet low entries; a single reachable top note cannot establish a usable tessitura.

  1. Seated: record three full hook takes with an instrumental, dry vocal capture and no tuned lead in playback. Ask each singer to flag effort and breath pressure.
  2. Moving: repeat three takes through the proposed blocking, with a 45-second rest between takes. Keep the guide melody and tempo identical.
  3. Compare: if the upper phrase repeatedly tightens, audition one semitone lower. If low entries repeatedly lose clarity, audition one higher. Retest the entire hook; revise a sustained note or breath point when transposition creates a new problem.
  4. Accept: three consecutive movement takes preserve the melody, readable words and planned breaths without reported strain or an unplanned octave change. The singer and vocal director sign off the comfortable repeated-note zone.

F# minor and the B-minor backup remain candidates. Do not assign final key, octave or upper harmony from public clips alone.

03 / Proposed wardrobe build

Two silhouettes. One stage code.

The flats are original design proposals for SAME SIDE. Existing editorials establish the pair's fashion range; they are references, not confirmed stage outfits or brand partnerships.

LIZ / Longline ivory

Silhouette: sleeveless longline tunic, shaped waist and an asymmetric lower panel over fitted stage shorts. Keep the shoulder clean and the vertical seam readable in the two-shot.

Material: sample a medium-weight stretch cady for body and recovery. Line the torso with a breathable stretch lining; choose a closely matched opaque knit for the shorts.

Construction: reinforce the panel junction, conceal the side closure, bind the neckline and armholes, and provide a separate reinforced wireless-pack pocket. Set the panel length during a shoe-on fitting.

REI / Cropped ink

Silhouette: short-sleeved cropped crew neck with a high-waist skort. A fixed asymmetric overlay creates a compact layer; secure the top-to-waist relationship through the full movement range.

Material: sample a dense fine-rib stretch knit with recovery for the top, matte stretch suiting for the overlay and an opaque stretch lining for the fitted shorts.

Construction: stabilize the neckline and waistband, stitch the overlay in place, and conceal closures away from the contact side. Provide wireless-pack access without lifting the costume's outer layer.

Shared finish

Use the same two parallel silver-tone bars at the waist, with rounded edges and a soft backing. Match scale, spacing and finish across the pair; request a brushed option if the show light creates glare. Fit both looks with secure dance footwear selected after a movement test.

Hair: LIZ uses a smooth low tie with the front secured; REI uses a compact shape tucked clear of the eyes. Fit wireless and in-ear routing before the final set. Makeup: keep both complexions controlled under the key light and use muted rose lips; LIZ has a soft eye contour, REI a more defined lash line. Check transfer and sweat resistance during rehearsal.

Fitting and camera approval

  1. Movement: record full arm lift, squat, lunge, pivot and the final-hook phrase in the complete look. Check coverage, waistband recovery, hem clearance and hardware contact.
  2. Camera: shoot the front two-shot and the vertical hook cut under show lighting. Check ivory clipping, knit moiré, silver reflections and the visibility of each silhouette at a wide framing.
  3. Release to stage: confirm the revised sample with styling, performance and the artists. Record final adjustments, pack access, duplicate items and quick-repair supplies.

Material, length, footwear and hair remain sample decisions until the movement and camera checks pass. No outfit is represented as commissioned, supplied or approved.

04 / Proposed pilot protocol

Three signals. Two markets. One release.

Treat the first stage as an activity test. A 72-hour read opens the review; it does not automatically commit the artist to a release.

Before the reveal

Artist strategy owns the brief, comparison set and go/hold decision. A&R owns the demo, vocal test and master readiness. Performance, content and marketing teams confirm rehearsal, capture and reporting capacity.

Reserve two feasible tour touchpoints in different markets only after management confirms the group calendar and rehearsal capacity. No existing tour date is represented as a confirmed unit slot.

Minimum production scope

One song, one stage blocking, one LED line system, one styling code. Capture a two-shot master and vertical-safe angles. Keep a fixed rehearsal allocation; defer a standalone MV and a full unit campaign until the gate passes.

The proposal assumes access to existing tour production and official-channel analytics, including market-level organic viewer counts for the comparison clips. Costs require actual vendor quotes and internal allocation.

  1. Reveal: special unreleased stage; separate paths converge at the final hook.
  2. Capture: allow organic fan discovery, then publish one 12-18 second official hook cut. No release-date promise.
  3. Read: take matching 24-hour and 72-hour snapshots after each official cut. Recheck both markets at day seven.
  4. Decide: artist strategy reviews the three demand signals with A&R, marketing and management. Missing evidence means Hold.

What counts as demand

01 / Hook reuse
Distinct creators making a new performance, singing or interpretation of the original hook. Report creators per 1,000 organic viewers of the corresponding official cut. Assign creators and viewers to the same independently verified market, platform and observation window; a missing market-level denominator means Hold. Deduplicate reposts and exclude official, paid and coordinated campaign accounts.
02 / Pair keyword growth
Mean daily unique authors discussing LIZ + REI, using the same fixed query, platform and verified market before and after reveal. Exclude paid, coordinated, repost-only, group-only and shipping-only discussion in both windows. Separately tag authors explicitly discussing the proposed song, hook or performance and report their share.
03 / Release requests
Unique users explicitly asking for this song's studio or digital release. Report users per 1,000 organic viewers of the corresponding official cut, using the same market, platform and observation window. A missing denominator means Hold.

Comparison and evidence quality

Before launch, select five recent official performance clips on the same channel with similar duration, promotion and tour context. Freeze the comparison set. Compare each post at the same elapsed age, using medians rather than the highest-performing clip.

Hook creators and release-request users per 1,000 organic viewers must exceed the matched median in both markets. Keep numerator and exposure denominator on the same platform, verified market and elapsed window. Treat these as exposure-normalized signals, not measured viewer conversion rates.

For pair discussion, compare the mean daily author count in the first 72 hours with the seven-day pre-reveal daily mean. Require growth in both markets and a majority of eligible post-window author-days to be song-specific: sum song-specific daily authors / sum all eligible daily pair authors must exceed 50%. An author counts once per day in each total. Freeze this proposed majority rule before reveal. It is a working gate chosen to make original-song interest central to the decision. Growth coinciding with song discussion does not establish causality.

At day seven, recompute the pair daily mean and song-specific share over the seven post-reveal days. Recalculate both response rates with seven-day exposure and compare with the frozen clips at the same elapsed age. All three signals must still pass.

Use platform audience geography or independently verified local channels. Language alone does not establish a market. Low counts, missing denominators or incomplete geography cannot be treated as a pass.

Go: all three demand signals pass in two markets, evidence checks pass, and the artists, master and activity calendar are approved. Schedule the digital single.

Hold: weak or incomplete evidence, a single market/channel spike, or response driven by group activity. Keep the stage tour-only and collect another observation.

Build: pair interest is strong while hook reuse and song requests remain weak. Commission a revised demo, then repeat the pilot. Do not expand the current song's release budget.

After Go

Release the single with a performance video, visualizer, recording behind and short pair interview. Reuse the stage's visual code. Prepare assets during the pilot only within the approved scope; publish the release date after the decision.

At 28 days after release, review saves per listener, repeat listening where available, continuing creator reuse and response to unit content against comparable releases. Return the strongest arrangement and content format to IVE's group activity. Commission a follow-up only after repeat demand and schedule capacity are established.

All thresholds and windows above are proposed working rules. They are not Starship internal standards or measured campaign results. The decision remains a cross-team review, not an automated score.
