01 / A&R brief

Velvet 2-Step Pop.

Write a close, rhythmic unit song built around two distinct voices. REI establishes character in the opening eight seconds. LIZ opens the melodic space. Their first shared hook makes the pair legible.

Musical specification

Tempo / meter
114 BPM preferred; 112-116 BPM range. 4/4.
Key candidates
F# minor with an A-major color in the hook. Backup: B minor / D major. Confirm tessitura with guide vocals before locking the key.
Verse
F#m9 / Dmaj7 / Aadd9 / E6
Pre-chorus
Dmaj7 / E6 / C#m7 / F#m9
Chorus
Aadd9 / E/G# / F#m9 / Dmaj7

Chord colors are a writing lane, not a mandatory harmonic template or a required modulation.

Production specification

Drums
Broken 2-step kick, shuffled closed hats, soft garage snare. Verse rim-click detail; wider clap in the final hook.
Bass
Elastic sub. Short syncopation under REI; longer notes under LIZ's pre-chorus.
Texture
Filtered pads, one glassy pluck motif, low-level vocal chops and light tape air.
Space
Close lead vocals, restrained reverb tails, separation around consonants. No oversized riser or heavy drop.

LIZ / Line & lift

Clean lead and longer melodic phrases. Widen doubles in the pre-chorus. Add an airy upper harmony and one restrained ad-lib in the final hook.

REI / Texture & hook

Dry, conversational lead; rap-sung phrasing in verse two. Use a subtle lower-color double and whispered tags without obscuring her natural tone.

76 bars / 2:40 at 114 BPM

Rounded timestamps below. The bar count is the session reference. At 112-116 BPM, this form runs approximately 2:43-2:37.

  1. 0:004 barsREI close intro. No full drums.
  2. 0:088 barsREI verse. Quiet 2-step hats enter.
  3. 0:258 barsLIZ pre-lift. Chord voicing opens.
  4. 0:428 barsHook one. Call and response; first shared phrase.
  5. 0:598 barsREI verse two. Denser rhythm.
  6. 1:168 barsLIZ pre-chorus. Longer melodic lift.
  7. 1:338 barsHook two. Both voices enter sooner.
  8. 1:498 barsBridge. Beat strips back; short phrases trade.
  9. 2:0616 barsFinal hook. Same phrase, distinct octave or texture. Alternate the last two bars; end at 2:40.

Hook direction

Different taste, same side
You go left, I go right
Still we meet on the same line

Draft writing prompt, not final lyrics. The hook should make sense without an explanation of the members' relationship.

Reference functions

  • IVE / Off The Record: private, elegant temperature.
  • aespa / Thirsty: smooth vocal layering.
  • NMIXX / Passionfruit: light UKG bounce.
  • PinkPantheress: close vocal and compact movement.

These are listening directions, not templates for melody, chords, or signature sounds.

Supply request

Request three full topline demos from writers with R&B vocal and 2-step programming experience. Each submission must include a two-voice guide, a 15-second hook cut, lyric sheet, BPM/key/bar map, writer contacts and split proposal.

Shortlist on role contrast, live singability, hook recall, and replay value. Run a seated vocal check and a movement rehearsal before the final selection.

Approved-demo delivery

Full mix, instrumental, clean vocal guide and aligned stems: 24-bit / 48 kHz WAV from bar one. Include dry lead/doubles, harmony stacks and a sample/interpolation declaration. Keep the concert playback version separate from the streaming master.

No title-track imitation, inserted rap break, heavy drop or twin-voice treatment. Key, tempo and budget remain subject to artist and production approval.

02 / Proposed pilot protocol

Three signals. Two markets. One release.

Treat the first stage as an activity test. A 72-hour read opens the review; it does not automatically commit the artist to a release.

Before the reveal

Artist strategy owns the brief, comparison set and go/hold decision. A&R owns the demo, vocal test and master readiness. Performance, content and marketing teams confirm rehearsal, capture and reporting capacity.

Reserve two feasible tour touchpoints in different markets only after management confirms the group calendar and rehearsal capacity. No existing tour date is represented as a confirmed unit slot.

Minimum production scope

One song, one stage blocking, one LED line system, one styling code. Capture a two-shot master and vertical-safe angles. Keep a fixed rehearsal allocation; defer a standalone MV and a full unit campaign until the gate passes.

The proposal assumes access to existing tour production and official-channel analytics. Costs require actual vendor quotes and internal allocation.

  1. Reveal: special unreleased stage; separate paths converge at the final hook.
  2. Capture: allow organic fan discovery, then publish one 12-18 second official hook cut. No release-date promise.
  3. Read: take matching 24-hour and 72-hour snapshots after each official cut. Recheck both markets at day seven.
  4. Decide: artist strategy reviews the three demand signals with A&R, marketing and management. Missing evidence means Hold.

What counts as demand

01 / Hook reuse
Distinct creators making a new performance, singing or interpretation of the original hook. Deduplicate reposts and exclude official, paid and coordinated campaign accounts.
02 / Pair keyword growth
Unique authors making song-specific LIZ + REI mentions, relative to the same markets' seven-day pre-reveal baseline. Generic group and shipping-only mentions do not qualify.
03 / Release requests
Unique users explicitly asking for this song's studio or digital release. Report per 1,000 organic viewers when that denominator is available.

Comparison and evidence quality

Before launch, select five recent official performance clips on the same channel with similar duration, promotion and tour context. Freeze the comparison set. Compare each post at the same elapsed age, using medians rather than the highest-performing clip.

Hook reuse and release-request rates must exceed the matched median in both markets. Pair mentions must exceed their pre-reveal baseline in both markets. All three must remain directionally positive at the day-seven review.

Use platform audience geography or independently verified local channels. Language alone does not establish a market. Low counts, missing denominators or incomplete geography cannot be treated as a pass.

Go: all three demand signals pass in two markets, evidence checks pass, and the master plus artist calendar are approved. Schedule the digital single.

Hold: weak or incomplete evidence, a single market/channel spike, or response driven by group activity. Keep the stage tour-only and collect another observation.

Build: pair interest is strong while hook reuse and song requests remain weak. Commission a second demo, then repeat the pilot. Do not expand the current song's release budget.

After Go

Release the single with a performance video, visualizer, recording behind and short pair interview. Reuse the stage's visual code. Prepare assets during the pilot only within the approved scope; publish the release date after the decision.

At 28 days after release, review saves per listener, repeat listening where available, continuing creator reuse and response to unit content against comparable releases. Return the strongest arrangement and content format to IVE's group activity. Commission a follow-up only after repeat demand and schedule capacity are established.

All thresholds and windows above are proposed working rules. They are not Starship internal standards or measured campaign results. The decision remains a cross-team review, not an automated score.
